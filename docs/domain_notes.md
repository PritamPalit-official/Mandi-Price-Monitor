# Domain Notes: Indian Agricultural Wholesale Markets (Mandi Data)

## Key Actors & Buyers
- **APMC Mandis (Regulated Wholesale Markets):** Central trading points established under state Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) Acts where farmers bring harvested produce to commission agents (*arhtiyas*) and licensed wholesalers.
- **Bulk Buyers & Institutional Procurement:** Food processors, retail chains (supermarkets, quick-commerce), hotel/restaurant/catering (HORECA) buyers, and bulk aggregators procurement teams who monitor daily modal prices to manage procurement costs.

## Price Drivers & Seasonality
1. **Monsoon & Crop Cycles:**
   - **Kharif (Monsoon), Late Kharif, and Rabi (Winter)** production windows dictate the supply cadence.
   - Example: Onions have three seasonal harvests (Kharif, Late Kharif, Rabi). Rabi harvest (stored in *chawls* across Nashik/Ahmednagar, Maharashtra) accounts for ~65% of annual production and feeds the country from May to October.
   - *Source:* National Horticultural Research and Development Foundation (NHRDF) / Ministry of Consumer Affairs.

2. **Supply Chain Losses & Perishability:**
   - Fresh staples (especially tomatoes and onions) are highly perishable. Post-harvest storage losses during monsoon moisture or extreme summer heat trigger sudden supply crunches and sharp price spikes.
   - Tomatoes face extreme shelf-life limits (3-5 days under ambient conditions), creating severe price volatility compared to storable root crops.
   - *Source:* ICAR - Indian Institute of Horticultural Research (IIHR).

3. **Inter-State Logistics & Regional Arbitrage:**
   - Production is concentrated geographically (e.g., Maharashtra & Karnataka for onions; Madhya Pradesh & Uttar Pradesh for potatoes; Kolar, Madanapalle, and Nashik for tomatoes).
   - Fuel prices, interstate transport strikes, and transit delays directly expand the spatial price spread between producing hubs and consumption metros.
   - *Source:* Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, Directorate of Marketing & Inspection (DMI).

4. **Government Interventions & Trade Policy:**
   - Minimum Export Price (MEP), export bans, import facilitation, and buffer stock releases (via NAFED/NCCF under the Price Stabilization Fund) create sudden administrative inflection points in wholesale benchmark prices.
   - *Source:* Department of Consumer Affairs, Government of India.

5. **Reporting Characteristics in AGMARKNET:**
   - **Modal Price:** The single most common price point at which the highest volume was traded that day (not a mathematical mean).
   - **Arrivals:** Daily recorded tonnage arriving at APMC market yards. Variations in reporting compliance mean daily market counts fluctuate.
   - *Source:* AGMARKNET Portal Documentation (agmarknet.gov.in).
